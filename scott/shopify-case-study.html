Building "Capalytics" at Scale

A case study in unifying enterprise capacity management, shifting engineering culture, and architecting for 1.4 billion daily records.

The Challenge: Enterprise Fragmentation

At JPMorgan Chase, infrastructure data was siloed. I was tasked with solving a massive data fragmentation problem where 7 global Lines of Business (LOBs) used different tools and metrics for capacity planning.

The primary strategic objective was to standardize the capacity management approach, tooling, and reporting across the enterprise. The goal was to establish a "single source of truth" so that reporting up to the Executive CIO was consistent, accurate, and comparable across the board.

1.4 Billion Rows of Data / Day
1,400+ Active Users
40% Efficiency Gain

My Role: Product & Technical Leadership

I served as the Global Product Owner and Lead Architect, managing a distributed team of 21 engineers and SREs across the US, India, and the UK. My leadership focused on two parallel tracks:

  • Technical Architecture: I architected the system to ingest telemetry from diverse sources (Verum, Satori, SAN arrays) into a unified view. I designed the system for high availability, configuring it to persist independently between two data centers with automated quarterly failovers.
  • Cultural Transformation: I built the SRE and DevOps teams from the ground up, driving a shift from Waterfall to Agile with 2-week sprints and mature CI/CD pipelines.

The Outcome

The project successfully replaced a rigid legacy vendor solution (CA Capacity Manager), eliminating significant licensing costs and technical debt. Key wins included:

  • Standardization: We established Capalytics as the standard tool across all LOBs, delivering 25+ custom views to support unified adoption.
  • Automation: By automating data correlation, we achieved a 40% time reduction in Resiliency Enhanced Testing.
  • Scale: The platform achieved a "Mature" rating in the firm’s strict Application Tollgate Process.

Retrospective: What I Would Change

If I were to execute this today, I would enforce an "API-First" strategy from Day 1. Initially, we focused on building custom UI views to secure stakeholder buy-in. While this achieved our standardization goals, it created a bottleneck where my core team had to manually build every new visualization.

Midway through, I pivoted the roadmap to establish standardized API extensions. Had I prioritized this "platform" mindset earlier, we could have unlocked internal developer velocity faster, allowing LOBs to build their own tools on top of our data engine immediately.
